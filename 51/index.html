51 Khutba

Artificial Intelligence: Building With Purpose, Rooted In Faith

First khutba · Opening

إِنَّ الْحَمْدَ لِلَّهِ نَحْمَدُهُ وَنَسْتَعِينُهُ وَنَسْتَغْفِرُهُ وَنَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ مِنْ شُرُورِ أَنْفُسِنَا وَمِنْ سَيِّئَاتِ أَعْمَالِنَا مَن يَهْدِهِ اللَّهُ فَلَا مُضِلَّ لَهُ وَمَن يُضْلِلْ فَلَا هَادِيَ لَهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَن لَّا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ صَلَّىٰ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ

All praise is due to Allah. We praise Him, we seek His help, we seek His forgiveness, and we seek refuge in Allah from the evil within ourselves and our evil deeds. Whoever Allah guides, there is none to misguide him. Whoever Allah leads astray, there is none to guide him. I testify there is no god but Allah alone, without any partners, and that Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, is His servant and His messenger.

Khutbat al-Hajah · Sunan an-Nasa’i 1404

يَٰأَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَقُولُوا قَوْلًا سَدِيدًا يُصْلِحْ لَكُمْ أَعْمَالَكُمْ وَيَغْفِرْ لَكُمْ ذُنُوبَكُمْ وَمَن يُطِعِ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ فَقَدْ فَازَ فَوْزًا عَظِيمًا

O you who have believed! Fear Allah and speak words of appropriate justice. He will correct for you your deeds and forgive you your sins. And whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger has certainly attained a great success.

أمَّا بعدُ، فإنَّ أصدقَ الحديثِ كتابُ اللهِ، وخيرَ الهديِ هديُ محمَّدٍ صلَّى اللهُ عليه وسلَّم، وشرَّ الأمورِ مُحدَثاتُها، وكلَّ مُحدَثةٍ بدعةٌ، وكلَّ بدعةٍ ضلالةٌ، وكلَّ ضلالةٍ في النَّارِ

To proceed: As for what follows: the truest speech is the Book of Allah, and the best guidance is the guidance of Muhammad, may Allah bless him and grant him peace. The worst of matters are those newly introduced (into the religion); every newly introduced matter is an innovation, every innovation is misguidance, and every misguidance is in the Fire.

Sunan an-Nasa’i 1578

1Are we allowed to build with this?

A massive shift is running through our workplaces, our schools, and our dinner tables.

  • Software that summarizes hundreds of books in seconds.
  • Engines that write code, diagnose imaging, and generate synthetic voices and video.
  • We call it Artificial Intelligence.

When rapid technological disruption hits, hesitation is a normal human response.

  • Some wonder if working with these tools conflicts with our spiritual values.
  • Others look at deepfakes and automated personas, wondering if the entire enterprise is spiritually destructive.
Pause · 3s

Our sacred tradition gives us a clear, grounded anchor.

  • Our jurists established a core principle:
  • Al-aslu fil-ashya’ al-ibahah.
  • In worldly matters, technology, and systems, the default ruling is permissibility.

Everything in this universe is open for us to explore, understand, and build, unless there is a clear, explicit divine prohibition.

  • Worship is bounded.
  • We cannot invent a fifth daily prayer.
  • We cannot add an extra cycle to Fajr.
  • In matters of ritual faith (‘ibadah), innovation is rejected.
  • But in matters of this worldly life (dunya), the universe is an open workshop.

Consider how the Prophet ﷺ handled this directly with his companions.

  • He walked past the farmers of Madinah while they were manually pollinating their date-palm trees.
  • He wondered aloud whether leaving them to natural winds might suffice.
  • Out of deep reverence, the companions stopped pollinating.
  • That season, the date yield fell sharply.
  • When they returned to him and mentioned the lost harvest, the Prophet ﷺ did not scold them for experimenting.
  • Instead, he declared a lasting principle:

أَنْتُمْ أَعْلَمُ بِأَمْرِ دُنْيَاكُمْ

“You know best regarding the affairs of your worldly life.”

Sahih Muslim 2363
  • He gave the Ummah full permission to observe, measure, test, fail, iterate, and invent.
  • Worldly technology is not an innovation in religion.
  • It is a capability gifted to human minds by Allah to solve practical problems.

2Is this new territory for us?

When Muslims led intellectually, we did not run away from worldly tools.

  • We invented them.
  • We refined them.
Transition

Think back to the fifth year of the Hijrah: the Battle of the Trench (Al-Khandaq).

  • An allied confederation of ten thousand enemy troops marched to destroy Madinah.
  • The Muslim defenders inside the city numbered only three thousand.
  • Outnumbered more than three to one, the defensive challenge was severe.

Salman al-Farsi, may Allah be pleased with him, stepped forward.

  • He brought a military engineering technique foreign to Arabia:
  • “In Persia, when we faced an overwhelming force, we dug an impassable trench around our defenses.”
  • The Prophet ﷺ did not dismiss the idea as foreign or un-Islamic.
  • He saw an effective worldly tool that preserved innocent life.
  • He embraced it immediately.
  • He picked up a pickaxe and dug into the rock with his own hands alongside his companions.

That practical mindset drove Muslim civilization for centuries.

  • In ninth-century Baghdad, Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi formulated algebra in Kitab al-Jabr wal-Muqabala.
  • His latinized name became the root for our modern word: Algorithm.
  • The step-by-step logic running inside every phone, database, and neural network today builds directly on that heritage.

In eleventh-century Cairo, Hasan Ibn al-Haytham was placed under house arrest.

  • He noticed a thin ray of light entering a darkened room through a tiny hole, projecting an inverted image of the world outside onto the wall opposite.
  • He documented the chamber, giving it the Arabic name:
  • Al-Qamrah (الْقَمْرَةُ).
  • Al-qamrah literally means a dark room or private chamber.
  • That Arabic word gave rise to the Latin camera obscura, which became our modern English word: Camera.
  • It established the foundations of optics, photography, digital displays, and computer vision.

These scholars were not secular figures distancing themselves from faith.

  • They were believers who understood that studying and mastering creation is a form of purposeful stewardship.
  • We did not trail the world.
  • We set the standard.

3What happens when we hold back?

What changed?

  • A moment arrived when our posture shifted from confident building to defensive fear.
  • Consider the historical adoption of the printing press.
Pause · 3s

In the mid-fifteenth century, Johannes Gutenberg introduced movable type to Europe.

  • It made books accessible to ordinary people.
  • In the Ottoman lands, leadership reacted with intense caution.
  • In 1485, Sultan Bayezid II issued an imperial edict prohibiting Muslims from using the printing press for Arabic script.
  • In 1515, Sultan Selim I reaffirmed that restriction.
  • The reservations were clear:
    • Protect tens of thousands of scribes and calligraphers whose trade depended on handwritten manuscripts.
    • Religious scholars feared that mechanical presses might dishonor sacred texts or introduce errors into the Qur’an.

Non-Muslim communities in the empire printed in Hebrew and Armenian, but the ban on Muslim-run printing of Arabic and Ottoman script lasted centuries.

  • It was not until 1727, nearly three hundred years after Gutenberg, that Ibrahim Muteferrika obtained authorization to open the first Ottoman press for non-religious works.
  • In those intervening centuries, Europe printed hundreds of millions of books.
  • Literacy exploded, scientific discovery accelerated, and whole societies reorganized.
  • We fell behind on the frontier of knowledge because we treated an information-sharing tool as an existential danger.
  • When an Ummah pulls back in fear, history does not pause.
  • The world moves forward.
  • If we retreat from modern computational tools, we will spend decades playing catch-up to systems that others decided to build.

4Does that mean we accept it blindly?

Engaging modern technology does not mean blind acceptance.

  • A tool reflects the intent of the hand holding it.
  • A surgical scalpel can save a life or cause fatal harm.
  • The instrument is neutral; the human being carries moral responsibility.
Pause · 3s

AI is not an existential savior, and it is not Dajjal.

  • It is an advanced mathematical pattern engine.
  • It has no soul (ruh), no conscience, and no moral awareness.
  • It does not fear Allah.
  • Human developers and users carry full accountability for its application.

Because the tool is powerful, we must study its real harms and establish firm ethical guardrails:

First: The Erosion of Truth.

  • We can now clone voices and generate photorealistic events that never happened.
  • Family members receive forwarded videos on messaging groups and accept them without question.
  • Allah warned us in Surah Al-Hujurat 49:6:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِنْ جَاءَكُمْ فَاسِقٌ بِنَبَإٍ فَتَبَيَّنُوا

“O you who believe, if an unverified report comes to you, investigate and verify.”

Surah Al-Hujurat 49:6
  • When media is easily fabricated, our standard of verification (tabayyun) must be higher.
  • Do not share or react until you verify original sources.

Second: Intellectual Laziness.

  • If our young people hand over writing, critical inquiry, and analytical reasoning to a prompt, their minds weaken.
  • Allah honored human beings with intellect (‘aql), reflection (tadabbur), and moral discernment.
  • Use technology to assist your research, never to bypass your own thinking.

Third: Protecting the Sacred.

  • A conversational bot is not a mufti.
  • It processes patterns in text; it lacks wisdom, empathy, and fear of Allah.
  • For life-altering choices - your marriage, family responsibilities, deep ethical questions - turn to living scholars who understand context and fear standing before their Creator.

5So what do we do this week?

If our families, students, and professionals leave this technology alone, who sets the standards?

  • People who build systems without spiritual grounding, without ethical restraint, and without accountability to Allah.
  • We need believers designing the models, auditing bias, improving medical diagnostics, and establishing data privacy protections.

Take three concrete commitments into your week:

  1. Explore AI openly with your family.
    • Sit down with your children or younger siblings this weekend.
    • Open an AI model together.
    • Test it on a topic you know well, observe where it is accurate, and identify where it makes errors.
    • Help them become thoughtful evaluators and creators, not passive consumers.
  2. Apply one tool to improve your daily craft.
    • In your office, school, clinic, or business, identify a routine task where automation reduces busywork.
    • Make your intention clear: gaining time to serve your family, help your community, and work with excellence.
  3. Guard your intellect and your worship.
    • Never let digital systems replace genuine reading, real human conversations, or quiet reflection.
    • Keep your worship focused and your conscience awake.
    • Use the tool; never let the tool use you.

Al-Baqarah 2:286
Concluding duas
  • Ya Allah, Ya ‘Alim, grant us knowledge that benefits our families and our communities, and protect us from knowledge that carries no benefit.
  • Ya Allah, Ya Hakim, grant us wisdom to discern truth from falsehood, and give us clarity in an age of synthetic deception and confusion.
  • Ya Allah, Ya Rashid, guide our hands and minds to master the tools of this world to serve Your creation, and keep our intentions firmly anchored in Your pleasure.
  • Ya Allah, Ya Qawiyy, inspire our youth to lead with excellence, to become ethical pioneers of beneficial science and technology, and to carry moral clarity into their work.
  • Ya Allah, Ya Hafiz, protect the intellects and hearts of our children from mental laziness and spiritual heedlessness.
  • Ya Allah, Ya Hadi, keep our hearts humble before Your majesty, and never allow our technical capability to blind us to our complete dependence upon You.
